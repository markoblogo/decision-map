# Full Run Example: FMCG Route-to-Market Expansion

## Scenario

**Company:** NorthMile Dairy  
**Sector:** chilled dairy / FMCG  
**Current footprint:** Western and Central Europe

NorthMile Dairy sells premium protein yogurts and drinkable kefir through supermarkets and convenience chains. The company is evaluating whether to expand into the Gulf through a route-to-market redesign focused on the UAE and Saudi Arabia.

The team faces a real trade-off:
- premium dairy demand exists, but chilled shelf access is expensive
- local importers want margin-heavy exclusive arrangements
- cold-chain service quality varies by channel
- the company can fund either a disciplined phased entry or a faster multi-channel push, not both

## Stage 0: Scope Check

- **Scope result:** In scope
- **Rewritten decision statement:** How should NorthMile expand into the Gulf over the next 9 months without damaging margin quality or cold-chain brand trust?
- **Decision owner:** Chief Commercial Officer
- **Primary objective:** establish a repeatable route-to-market in UAE and Saudi with reorder-ready economics
- **Obvious constraints:**
  - first-year launch budget cap: EUR 2.2M
  - no local operating team today
  - chilled distribution requires reliable cold-chain partners
  - shelf-life limits make waste risk material
- **Unresolved blockers:** final landed-cost sensitivity by channel and acceptable exclusivity structure

## Stage 1: Intake

### User-provided context

- NorthMile's strongest products are high-protein yogurts with clean-label positioning.
- Brand performs well with health-conscious urban consumers and premium grocery chains.
- Two regional importers have shown interest; both request exclusivity for at least one country.
- Existing factory capacity can support the first-year Gulf plan without capex.
- Current leadership believes the biggest risk is margin erosion through discounts and spoilage rather than lack of demand.

### Intake output

#### Known facts

- Launch budget: EUR 2.2M.
- No local entity or local team.
- Product requires disciplined cold-chain handling.
- Importers are available, but both want exclusivity.
- Factory capacity is available for phase one.

#### Assumptions

- Premium health-positioned dairy can win in affluent urban channels.
- Convenience and food-service may be attractive later, but should not lead phase one.
- One-country proof may be strategically cleaner than simultaneous regional rollout.

#### Interpretations

- Leadership's real concern is protecting premium economics, not just entering quickly.
- Importers may use exclusivity demands to maximize leverage before proving execution quality.

#### Missing information

- Sell-through expectations by channel.
- Waste tolerance at distributor and retailer level.
- Relative power of modern trade vs premium convenience in the UAE.
- Whether Saudi expansion should be sequential or simultaneous.

#### Initial framing confidence

Medium

## Stage 2: Clarifying Questions

### Most decision-critical questions

1. Which market matters more first: UAE proof quality or Saudi scale?
2. How much margin deterioration is acceptable in the first 2 quarters?
3. What is the maximum acceptable spoilage/write-off rate?
4. How much commercial control is leadership willing to give an exclusive importer?
5. Is brand building more important than immediate volume?
6. What retailer or distributor behavior would count as a failed launch signal?
7. Which product SKUs travel best under Gulf cold-chain realities?
8. Can the team support frequent in-market commercial troubleshooting remotely?

### Condensed user answers

- UAE proof quality matters first; Saudi scale matters after channel learnings exist.
- Margin can dip, but gross margin must stay above 12% after waste and trade spend.
- Spoilage above 4% is unacceptable.
- Leadership can accept distributor exclusivity only with performance clauses and review gates.
- Brand and premium positioning matter more than fast volume.
- Failed launch signal: strong initial stocking but weak reorder discipline after 8 weeks.
- Smaller premium SKUs and drinkable formats are easiest to place first.
- Remote management is possible, but only for a narrow launch footprint.

### Framing confidence

Medium

## Stage 3: First Strategy Map

### Pre-map restatement

- **Decision statement:** How to enter the Gulf with sustainable premium RTM economics.
- **Known facts:** strong product-market fit in premium health positioning, constrained budget, importer interest, cold-chain complexity.
- **Key assumptions:** UAE is the right proving ground; premium discipline beats broad volume chase.
- **Major unknowns:** reorder velocity, spoilage control, and importer execution quality.
- **Framing confidence:** Medium

### Option A: UAE Premium Grocery First

- **Summary:** Launch in UAE only through premium grocery and select modern-trade chains with a narrow SKU set.
- **Expected upside:** cleanest learning environment, strongest brand control, lower channel sprawl.
- **Price / cost:** high listing cost, medium launch speed, lower dilution risk, moderate operational complexity.
- **Required resources:** premium retail account access, importer with refrigerated capability, brand sampling budget, field execution oversight.
- **Key risks:** low initial volume and importer frustration if sell-in is slower than expected.
- **Likely reactions:** retailers ask for more promo support; competitors defend with discounts and local shelf relationships.
- **Time effects:** slower start, stronger learning quality, better long-term brand control.
- **Core assumptions:** premium urban grocery shoppers will reorder if shelf execution is clean.
- **Breakpoints:** reorder from top chains absent by week 8; spoilage >4%.
- **Signals to monitor:** reorder cadence, spoilage, promo dependency, weighted gross margin.
- **Confidence:** Medium

### Option B: Dual-Market Importer Rollout

- **Summary:** Enter UAE and Saudi simultaneously through one regional importer with broad channel coverage.
- **Expected upside:** faster revenue scale and faster distributor commitment.
- **Price / cost:** high complexity, high working-capital demand, higher exclusivity risk, medium/high execution burden.
- **Required resources:** stronger importer governance, marketing funds, bilingual packaging readiness, broader demand planning.
- **Key risks:** weak control, diluted learning, poor cold-chain consistency across markets.
- **Likely reactions:** importer pushes for volume over discipline; retailers demand deeper promotions.
- **Time effects:** fast initial expansion, slower root-cause diagnosis if problems emerge.
- **Core assumptions:** one importer can execute consistently across both markets.
- **Breakpoints:** channel performance diverges with no corrective control; margin falls below 12%.
- **Signals to monitor:** country-by-country reorder, waste by market, discount intensity, fill-rate reliability.
- **Confidence:** Low

### Option C: UAE Proof + Performance-Gated Saudi Expansion

- **Summary:** Launch in UAE first with a performance-gated importer contract that unlocks Saudi expansion only after defined reorder and waste thresholds are met.
- **Expected upside:** combines disciplined learning with a built-in expansion path.
- **Price / cost:** medium/high contracting effort, medium launch speed, lower strategic regret.
- **Required resources:** tighter contract design, importer scorecard, phased channel plan, internal review cadence.
- **Key risks:** importer resists gating; expansion may slip if proof takes longer than planned.
- **Likely reactions:** importer negotiates for softer targets; competitors continue price-led defense but cannot force expansion timing.
- **Time effects:** deliberate first phase, stronger readiness for second market, more optionality preserved.
- **Core assumptions:** importer accepts governance in exchange for regional upside.
- **Breakpoints:** importer refuses enforceable performance clauses; UAE proof metrics fail to clear thresholds.
- **Signals to monitor:** sell-through by SKU, spoilage, net margin, importer service-level adherence.
- **Confidence:** High

### Option D: Food-Service and Hospitality Trial Before Retail

- **Summary:** Enter through hotels, gyms, and premium food-service to validate cold-chain execution before retail.
- **Expected upside:** controlled channel learning and lower listing-fee exposure.
- **Price / cost:** medium commercialization effort, slower consumer brand building, medium operational complexity.
- **Required resources:** specialty distributors, chef/fitness partnership activation, small-format B2B sales materials.
- **Key risks:** learnings may not transfer cleanly to retail; volume may remain too low for importer enthusiasm.
- **Likely reactions:** competitors may ignore early; importers may deprioritize if retail path stays vague.
- **Time effects:** safer cold-chain test, slower brand awareness, weaker retail proof.
- **Core assumptions:** execution quality matters more than immediate shelf presence.
- **Breakpoints:** channel remains too fragmented to create actionable proof; no clear retail signal emerges.
- **Signals to monitor:** service-level reliability, waste, repeat order rates, channel economics.
- **Confidence:** Medium

### Suggested shortlist

1. **UAE Proof + Performance-Gated Saudi Expansion**
2. **UAE Premium Grocery First**
3. **Food-Service and Hospitality Trial Before Retail**

### Best current working hypothesis

NorthMile should not pursue a simultaneous Gulf rollout. The strongest path is a UAE-first launch with performance-gated importer economics and explicit thresholds that unlock Saudi expansion only after reorder, waste, and gross-margin targets are met.

### What would change this view

- A high-quality importer agrees to dual-market governance with enforceable service clauses.
- Cold-chain waste in UAE proves structurally higher than expected even in premium grocery.
- Retailers show weak reorder discipline but food-service trials significantly outperform on reliability and margin.

## Stage 4: Strategy Selection and Deep Dive

### Selected options

1. UAE Proof + Performance-Gated Saudi Expansion
2. UAE Premium Grocery First

### Deep dive: UAE Proof + Performance-Gated Saudi Expansion

- **Strategic logic:** balances premium control with regional expansion optionality.
- **What must be true:** importer accepts staged governance; UAE channels deliver credible reorder signal within one quarter.
- **Execution path:**
  1. finalize gated importer contract
  2. limit launch to target UAE chains and 4-6 flagship SKUs
  3. run weekly scorecard on sell-through, spoilage, and fill rate
  4. unlock Saudi preparation only if thresholds hold for 8 weeks
- **Required resources:** legal/commercial negotiation support, trade-marketing playbook, weekly performance review owner.
- **Weak points:** importer may demand exclusivity without hard gates; internal patience may erode if volume starts slowly.
- **First tests:** 8-week UAE pilot with pre-agreed reorder and spoilage benchmarks.
- **Invalidators:** spoilage >4%, gross margin <12%, no reorder momentum from target accounts.
- **Updated confidence:** High

### Deep dive: UAE Premium Grocery First

- **Strategic logic:** maximizes brand discipline but keeps expansion optionality outside the contract structure.
- **Why it could work:** premium shoppers and premium retailers align with the brand's strongest positioning.
- **Why it could fail:** too much time and budget could be spent proving a narrow retail niche without creating scale options.
- **First tests:** compare reorder and promo-dependency in 2 premium chains vs target expectations.
- **Updated confidence:** Medium

## Stage 5: Decision Summary

### Chosen working strategy

**UAE Proof + Performance-Gated Saudi Expansion**

### Why it was chosen

- best balance of control, learning quality, and future expansion readiness
- preserves premium positioning without overcommitting to a dual-market rollout
- creates explicit review gates instead of relying on importer optimism

### Rejected or deferred options

- **Dual-Market Importer Rollout:** rejected for excessive complexity and low control.
- **Food-Service Trial First:** deferred as a fallback if retail proof quality is weak.
- **Pure UAE Premium Grocery First:** retained as a close variant, but weaker than the gated-expansion structure.

### Accepted trade-offs

- slower first-quarter volume in exchange for better margin discipline
- higher upfront contracting effort in exchange for lower strategic regret
- narrower initial footprint in exchange for cleaner signal quality

### Immediate next actions

1. Draft importer term sheet with performance gates, service levels, and review dates.
2. Lock a UAE launch SKU set and target account list.
3. Define weekly scorecard: reorder, spoilage, trade-spend intensity, fill rate, gross margin.
4. Set Saudi expansion criteria now, before launch pressure distorts the bar.

### Signals to monitor

- reorder rate after 8 weeks
- spoilage percentage by account
- gross margin after trade spend
- importer service-level adherence
- promo dependency vs baseline assumptions

### Revisit trigger

Reopen the decision immediately if spoilage exceeds 4%, importer governance slips, or top target accounts do not reorder by the agreed pilot checkpoint.

### What would change this view

- if UAE premium grocery underperforms but food-service shows superior economics, redirect the second-phase plan
- if a regional importer offers materially better governance and execution proof, re-evaluate simultaneous market coverage
