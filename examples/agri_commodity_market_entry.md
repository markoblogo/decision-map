# Example: Agri / Commodities Market Entry

## Scenario

**Company:** DniproSun Foods  
**Sector:** edible oils / agri exports  
**Current footprint:** Ukraine, Romania, Bulgaria, Turkey

DniproSun Foods is a mid-sized sunflower oil producer and packer evaluating whether to enter the Indian market over the next two quarters with bottled refined sunflower oil for urban retail and food-service distributors.

The decision is difficult because:
- India is a large import market but highly price-sensitive
- palm and soybean oils create constant substitution pressure
- landed cost volatility depends on Black Sea logistics, FX, and duty shifts
- the company can fund only one major market-entry bet this year
- local distributors want aggressive commercial terms before volume proof exists

## Decision statement

Should DniproSun enter India in the next 6 months, and if so through which market-entry strategy, given limited commercial capacity, freight volatility, and margin pressure from competing edible oils?

## Known facts

- DniproSun has annual export capacity for 42,000 additional tons without capex.
- Management allocated a maximum first-phase commercial budget of $1.4M.
- India imported large volumes of edible oils last year, but switching between sunflower, soybean, and palm remains common when relative pricing changes.
- The company has no local legal entity in India and no in-market sales team.
- Two regional distributors are interested, but both want 75-90 day payment terms and launch support.
- Gross margin floor for a new market program is 8% after logistics and trade spend.
- Current freight from Black Sea corridors remains more volatile than pre-war baselines.

## Key assumptions

- Urban retail buyers will pay a small premium for stable quality and food-safety credentials.
- Modern trade and premium grocery channels are more realistic than mass-market national rollout in phase one.
- Food-service distribution may scale faster than retail if the company accepts lower early brand visibility.
- Import duty or FX movement could erase the margin advantage within one quarter.

## Major unknowns

- Whether sunflower oil premium positioning is defendable if palm prices decline further.
- How much trade marketing support distributors truly need for a 6-month launch.
- Which channel reaches reorder velocity fastest: retail shelves, food-service, or private label.
- How strict the receivables risk controls must be for first shipments.

## Framing confidence

Medium

## First strategy map

### Option A: Premium Urban Retail Launch
- **Summary:** Enter via premium retail chains and e-commerce grocery in 6 metro areas with branded 1L and 5L packs.
- **Expected upside:** builds long-term brand equity and better unit margin if premium positioning holds.
- **Price / cost:**
  - money: high trade marketing and listing fees
  - time: medium/high
  - reputation: medium upside if quality lands well; downside if shelf turnover is weak
  - opportunity_cost: delays food-service scale learning
  - operational_complexity: high
  - customer_trust: potentially strong if certifications and origin story matter
  - strategic_flexibility_lost: medium
- **Required resources:** local importer/distributor, retail key-account support, in-market activation budget, working-capital discipline.
- **Key risks:** slow shelf rotation, distributor pushback on launch spend, premium positioning breaks under commodity repricing.
- **Likely reactions:** retail buyers demand stronger promos; commodity competitors respond through discounting rather than branding.
- **Time effects:** short-term slow sell-in, medium-term brand proof if repeat appears, long-term stronger differentiation.
- **Core assumptions:** urban consumers value quality and traceability enough to offset higher price.
- **Breakpoints:** weighted average gross margin <8%; reorder from top 3 chains absent after 10 weeks.
- **Signals to monitor:** shelf rotation by city, trade-spend per reorder, gross margin after rebates, repeat order cadence.
- **Confidence:** Low

### Option B: Food-Service Distributor Wedge
- **Summary:** Launch first through hotel, restaurant, and institutional food-service distributors using bulk and 15L packs.
- **Expected upside:** faster volume learning, fewer SKU complexities, lower brand-building burden.
- **Price / cost:**
  - money: medium
  - time: medium
  - reputation: low/medium
  - opportunity_cost: slower retail brand recognition
  - operational_complexity: medium
  - customer_trust: relationship-led rather than consumer-brand-led
  - strategic_flexibility_lost: low
- **Required resources:** B2B distributor contracts, credit control, pack configuration support, procurement-facing sales materials.
- **Key risks:** margin compression if buyers treat oil as a pure commodity; receivables exposure.
- **Likely reactions:** incumbent suppliers respond with temporary commercial concessions and bundled kitchen-supply terms.
- **Time effects:** short-term faster shipment velocity, medium-term procurement lock-in if service is reliable, long-term weaker consumer brand asset.
- **Core assumptions:** institutional buyers prioritize supply reliability and quality consistency more than origin branding.
- **Breakpoints:** net collection cycle exceeds 95 days; first two distributors fail to convert trial volume into repeat purchase.
- **Signals to monitor:** reorder interval, realized margin by pack size, overdue receivables, distributor forecast accuracy.
- **Confidence:** Medium

### Option C: Private Label Entry Through Regional Retailer
- **Summary:** Enter India under retailer-owned branding to win early volume with lower customer-acquisition cost.
- **Expected upside:** simpler path to volume and lower marketing spend.
- **Price / cost:**
  - money: medium
  - time: medium
  - reputation: low external visibility
  - opportunity_cost: no own-brand equity creation
  - operational_complexity: medium/high
  - customer_trust: depends on retailer brand, not DniproSun
  - strategic_flexibility_lost: high once capacity is tied to private label
- **Required resources:** retailer negotiation capability, packaging compliance, margin guardrails, supply consistency.
- **Key risks:** retailer bargaining power traps margins; difficult to switch back to branded route later.
- **Likely reactions:** competitors compete on price and supply assurance.
- **Time effects:** short-term fast volume if signed, medium-term dependence on one account, long-term limited strategic differentiation.
- **Core assumptions:** private-label buyers accept Ukrainian origin if quality and landed cost stay stable.
- **Breakpoints:** exclusive terms restrict future branded entry; contribution margin falls below hurdle after first quarter.
- **Signals to monitor:** account concentration, margin after deductions, service-level penalties, renewal leverage.
- **Confidence:** Medium

### Option D: Wait-and-Watch With Distributor Trials Only
- **Summary:** Delay a full market launch, run only limited commercial trials while monitoring duty, FX, and competitor price moves.
- **Expected upside:** preserves capital and reduces timing risk.
- **Price / cost:**
  - money: low
  - time: low/medium
  - reputation: low
  - opportunity_cost: may miss distributor window and first-mover credibility in target channel
  - operational_complexity: low
  - customer_trust: neutral
  - strategic_flexibility_lost: low
- **Required resources:** small trial inventory, monitoring cadence, distributor trial agreements.
- **Key risks:** paralysis; learning too little to support next-season decision.
- **Likely reactions:** distributors deprioritize DniproSun in favor of more committed suppliers.
- **Time effects:** short-term downside protection, medium-term slower market knowledge, long-term risk of lost access.
- **Core assumptions:** volatility will normalize enough to improve entry economics later.
- **Breakpoints:** trial partners disengage; freight or duty conditions do not improve despite delay.
- **Signals to monitor:** relative price spread vs palm/soybean, distributor responsiveness, trial conversion to committed orders.
- **Confidence:** Medium

## Suggested shortlist

1. **Food-Service Distributor Wedge**
- Best balance of speed, manageable complexity, and realistic first-phase commercial load.

2. **Private Label Entry Through Regional Retailer**
- Useful if the team prioritizes faster volume and can accept weaker strategic control.

3. **Wait-and-Watch With Distributor Trials Only**
- Sensible fallback if margin volatility worsens before contract signing.

## Working strategic hypothesis

DniproSun should avoid a broad branded retail launch in phase one and instead use a food-service distributor wedge with strict receivables guardrails, while negotiating one private-label option as a conditional secondary path if volume proof arrives faster than expected.

## What would change this view

- A sharp decline in palm oil pricing that makes sunflower premiums unsellable.
- A distributor willing to fund a premium retail launch with materially lower commercial support requirements.
- New trade-policy or FX moves that push landed margin below the internal floor even for food-service packs.
